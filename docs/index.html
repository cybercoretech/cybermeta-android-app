05 / HOW YOU KNOW IT WORKED

Scrubbed, then checked by someone else.

The engine is the same Rust code as the cybermeta command-line tool, compiled into the app.

1

Split

The file is cut into its blocks: picture data, EXIF, XMP, IPTC, colour profile, comments, thumbnails, trailers.

2

Rebuild

A new file is assembled from the picture data alone, copied byte for byte. Nothing is decoded or re-encoded.

3

Keep only what you chose

Rotation and colour profile go back in as brand-new blocks holding only those values.

4

Verify

A second, independent parser reads the result. If anything else is still there, you get an error, never a leaky photo.

07 / QUESTIONS

Fine print, in plain words.

Does Cybermeta upload my photos anywhere?

It can't. The app doesn't hold Android's INTERNET permission (it's explicitly removed in the manifest), so the system won't let it open a network connection. Photos are read, cleaned and handed back to the share sheet entirely on your phone.

Does cleaning reduce quality?

No. The compressed picture data is copied byte for byte into the new file; only the metadata blocks around it are left out. The one exception is HEIC/AVIF, which can't be cleaned that way yet: Cybermeta converts those to a high-quality JPEG (you can turn that off).

Why does it ask for "media location" access?

Android hides GPS from apps by default and hands them a copy with the coordinates zeroed. Without the permission the report would say "no location" for a photo that has one. The cleaned copy is safe either way; the permission only makes the report honest.

What do the clean modes do?

Share-safe removes everything but keeps the colour profile and rotation. Location only removes GPS and named places and keeps the rest. Anonymous camera removes everything, then labels the photo "Generic Camera". Pixels only keeps nothing at all. Shift dates moves every timestamp by an offset you choose.

My Samsung shoots motion photos. What happens to them?

A motion photo is a JPEG with a short video glued on after the end of the image, and that video can carry its own location and audio. A full clean removes it and keeps the still photo. "Location only" warns you that it's still there.

Can I overwrite my originals?

Yes, but only through Android's own "allow Cybermeta to modify these photos" prompt, for the exact photos you picked. By default Cybermeta never touches originals: it shares or saves clean copies to Pictures/Cybermeta.

What is a ghost thumbnail or cropping leak?

When you crop a photo in a gallery or editing app to remove a face, address, or sensitive detail, the app often saves the cropped image but leaves the full-frame original thumbnail inside EXIF IFD1. Anyone who extracts that thumbnail can see what you cropped out. Cybermeta audits embedded thumbnails, detects aspect ratio mismatches, displays a side-by-side comparison, and scrubs the thumbnail completely.

How does Cybermeta show city and country without internet?

Cybermeta bundles an ultra-compact offline database of ~34,000 world cities (GeoNames cities15000) directly inside the app. When coordinates are detected, an on-device nearest-neighbor search resolves the locality in under 2 milliseconds without sending a single byte across the network.

How does the Quick Settings tile work?

You can add the "Clean Latest" tile to your Android notification shade. Tapping it queries the most recent camera capture from your local MediaStore, opens Cybermeta, audits and scrubs it, and lets you immediately share or save the clean copy in seconds.
